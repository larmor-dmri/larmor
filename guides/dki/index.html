DKI#

Diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) measures how far the diffusion in each direction departs from a Gaussian, and gives maps of the mean, axial and radial kurtosis besides the DTI maps. It adds a kurtosis tensor W to the diffusion tensor D, and fits both to the log signal, log(S) = log(S0) - b g^T D g + b^2 MD^2 W(g) / 6 (Jensen et al. 2005). Kurtosis shows as the curvature of the log signal in b, so DKI needs at least two non-zero shells. Citing lists the papers.

The data on this page

The examples on this page run on a small synthetic image, which the code below writes to the current folder as dwi.nii.gz, with its gradient files dwi.bval and dwi.bvec and a mask, mask.nii.gz. The image has 12 x 12 x 2 voxels of 2 mm, two b=0 images and 30 directions at each of b=1000 and 2000 s/mm^2. Two bundles cross in its middle, one along x and one along y, and the other voxels hold isotropic diffusion. To work on your own data, use your files instead.

import numpy as np

from larmor.io import save_volume, write_bvals_bvecs
from larmor.sd import AnisotropicRF
from larmor.types import GradientTable, Volume

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
directions = rng.normal(size=(60, 3))
directions /= np.linalg.norm(directions, axis=1, keepdims=True)
bvals = np.r_[0, 0, [1000] * 30, [2000] * 30]
bvecs = np.r_[np.zeros((2, 3)), directions]
gtab = GradientTable(bvals, bvecs)

fibre = AnisotropicRF([1.7e-3, 0.3e-3, 0.3e-3], mean_kurtosis=0.5)
along_x, along_y = fibre.signal(bvals, gtab.bvecs, np.eye(3)[:2]).T
isotropic = np.exp(-bvals * 0.8e-3)

signal = np.broadcast_to(isotropic, (12, 12, 2, len(bvals))).copy()
signal[:, 4:8] = along_x
signal[4:8, :] = along_y
signal[4:8, 4:8] = (along_x + along_y) / 2
data = 1000 * signal + rng.normal(0, 10, signal.shape)

affine = np.diag([2.0, 2.0, 2.0, 1.0])
save_volume(Volume(data.astype(np.float32), affine), "dwi.nii.gz")
write_bvals_bvecs(gtab, "dwi.bval", "dwi.bvec", ref_path="dwi.nii.gz")
save_volume(Volume(np.ones((12, 12, 2), np.uint8), affine), "mask.nii.gz")

Fitting the tensors#

from larmor.dwi import DKIModel
from larmor.io import load_dwi, load_volume, save_volume

dwi, gtab = load_dwi("dwi.nii.gz", "dwi.bval", "dwi.bvec")
fit = DKIModel().fit(dwi, gtab, mask=load_volume("mask.nii.gz"))

save_volume(dwi.with_data(fit.mk), "mk.nii.gz")
print(f"MK in the bundles: {fit.mk[0, 5, 0]:.2f}")

The result, a DKIFit, holds the same maps as a DTI fit (fa, md, ad, rd, pe, eigen_vals, dt, npd). It also holds the mean, axial and radial kurtosis (mk, ak, rk), the kurtosis anisotropy (ka), the kurtosis tensor kt with its 15 elements, and the fitted signal at b=0, s0. All maps are zero outside the mask.

lar dki dwi.nii.gz dki --bvals dwi.bval --bvecs dwi.bvec -m mask.nii.gz

The command writes the DTI maps (dki_fa.nii.gz, dki_md.nii.gz, dki_ad.nii.gz, dki_rd.nii.gz, dki_pe.nii.gz, dki_npd.nii.gz) and the kurtosis maps dki_mk.nii.gz, dki_ak.nii.gz, dki_rk.nii.gz and dki_ka.nii.gz.

Options#

  • Fit method. The tensors are fitted by weighted least squares by default, with weights from the predicted signal (Veraart et al. 2013). DKIModel(fit_method="ols") or --fit ols uses ordinary least squares instead.

  • Constraints. By default the fit keeps the tensors physically plausible (Tabesh et al. 2011). In each direction n, the diffusivity D(n) and the kurtosis K(n) must satisfy D(n) >= 0, K(n) >= 0 and K(n) <= 3 / (b_max D(n)), where b_max is the largest b-value. Larmor imposes these constraints along 256 directions spread over the half sphere, whereas the paper uses the acquired gradient directions. A voxel whose unconstrained fit already meets them keeps that fit. DKIModel(constrained=False) or --unconstrained fits without the constraints. Voxels with a diffusivity near 0, such as those at the edge of the mask, can still give very large kurtosis values.

  • S0. Before the fit, each voxel’s b=0 signal is checked against its diffusion-weighted signal, and implausible b=0 values are replaced from neighbouring voxels, as estimate_s0() describes.

  • Tensors that are not positive definite. As for DTI, these voxels are marked in npd, and md is a third of the trace of the fitted diffusion tensor, the same MD by which the kurtosis tensor is normalised. The kurtosis maps are 0 in these voxels, since K(n) is not defined in every direction.

  • Mask and threads. As for DTI.
