DTI#

Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) gives maps of the fractional anisotropy (FA), the mean, axial and radial diffusivities (MD, AD, RD) and the principal direction of diffusion. It describes the diffusion in each voxel with a 3x3 tensor D, fitted to the log of the signal S measured at b-value b along direction g, log(S) = log(S0) - b g^T D g (Basser et al. 1994). The maps come from the tensor’s eigenvalues and its first eigenvector. Citing lists the papers, and Conventions gives the units and the tensor’s element order.

The data on this page

The examples on this page run on a small synthetic image, which the code below writes to the current folder as dwi.nii.gz, with its gradient files dwi.bval and dwi.bvec and a mask, mask.nii.gz. The image has 12 x 12 x 2 voxels of 2 mm, two b=0 images and 30 directions at each of b=1000 and 2000 s/mm^2. Two bundles cross in its middle, one along x and one along y, and the other voxels hold isotropic diffusion. To work on your own data, use your files instead.

import numpy as np

from larmor.io import save_volume, write_bvals_bvecs
from larmor.sd import AnisotropicRF
from larmor.types import GradientTable, Volume

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
directions = rng.normal(size=(60, 3))
directions /= np.linalg.norm(directions, axis=1, keepdims=True)
bvals = np.r_[0, 0, [1000] * 30, [2000] * 30]
bvecs = np.r_[np.zeros((2, 3)), directions]
gtab = GradientTable(bvals, bvecs)

fibre = AnisotropicRF([1.7e-3, 0.3e-3, 0.3e-3], mean_kurtosis=0.5)
along_x, along_y = fibre.signal(bvals, gtab.bvecs, np.eye(3)[:2]).T
isotropic = np.exp(-bvals * 0.8e-3)

signal = np.broadcast_to(isotropic, (12, 12, 2, len(bvals))).copy()
signal[:, 4:8] = along_x
signal[4:8, :] = along_y
signal[4:8, 4:8] = (along_x + along_y) / 2
data = 1000 * signal + rng.normal(0, 10, signal.shape)

affine = np.diag([2.0, 2.0, 2.0, 1.0])
save_volume(Volume(data.astype(np.float32), affine), "dwi.nii.gz")
write_bvals_bvecs(gtab, "dwi.bval", "dwi.bvec", ref_path="dwi.nii.gz")
save_volume(Volume(np.ones((12, 12, 2), np.uint8), affine), "mask.nii.gz")

Fitting the tensor#

from larmor.dwi import DTIModel
from larmor.io import load_dwi, load_volume, save_volume

dwi, gtab = load_dwi("dwi.nii.gz", "dwi.bval", "dwi.bvec")
mask = load_volume("mask.nii.gz")
fit = DTIModel().fit(dwi, gtab, mask=mask)

save_volume(dwi.with_data(fit.fa), "fa.nii.gz")
save_volume(dwi.with_data(fit.md), "md.nii.gz")
print(f"FA in the bundles: {fit.fa[0, 5, 0]:.2f}, elsewhere: {fit.fa[0, 0, 0]:.2f}")

The result, a DTIFit, holds the maps as arrays on the image’s grid, with zeros outside the mask. Besides fa, md, ad and rd, it holds the principal eigenvector pe, the eigenvalues eigen_vals, the tensor dt with its six elements in the order xx, xy, xz, yy, yz, zz, and the map npd described under Options. Diffusivities are in mm^2/s, since b-values are in s/mm^2. The principal eigenvector and the tensor are in world coordinates (RAS+), like the gradient table, not in the voxel frame of the image.

lar dti dwi.nii.gz dti --bvals dwi.bval --bvecs dwi.bvec -m mask.nii.gz

The second argument is the prefix of the output files. The command writes dti_fa.nii.gz, dti_md.nii.gz, dti_ad.nii.gz and dti_rd.nii.gz, the principal eigenvector dti_pe.nii.gz, and dti_npd.nii.gz, which is 1 in the voxels whose tensor is not positive definite. Diffusivities are in mm^2/s, since b-values are in s/mm^2, and the principal eigenvector is in world coordinates (RAS+), not in the voxel frame of the image.

Options#

  • Fit method. The tensor is fitted by ordinary least squares by default. DTIModel(fit_method="wls") or --fit wls fits it by weighted least squares instead, with weights from the predicted signal (Veraart et al. 2013).

  • Tensors that are not positive definite. Noise or artefacts can give a tensor with an eigenvalue at or below zero. Larmor does not correct these voxels. Their md is still a third of the tensor’s trace, and fa, ad, rd and eigen_vals come from the eigenvalues as fitted, with FA clipped to [0, 1]. The voxels are marked True in npd (1 in dti_npd.nii.gz) and their number is logged as a warning, so that you can leave them out of an analysis.

  • Mask. Voxels outside the mask are not fitted. Without a mask, Larmor computes a brain mask from the mean b=0 image, in the same way as create_brain_mask() and lar mask. On a small crop this mask can come out empty, and the fit then stops with an error; pass a mask, or compute one with a smaller morph.

  • Shells. The fit needs at least two distinct b-values. To fit one shell of a multi-shell acquisition, keep its volumes first, as in Gradient tables and shells.

  • Threads. The voxels are fitted in parallel on every core. n_jobs in Python and --nt on the command line set the number of threads, and the result does not depend on it.

Viewing the maps#

lar bv -v dti_fa.nii.gz -v dti_pe.nii.gz

The viewer can draw the principal eigenvector as direction colours weighted by FA, as shown in DTI colours in the viewer.
