class larmor.dwi.DKIFit(dt, kt, s0, fa, md, ad, rd, pe, eigen_vals, npd, mk, ak, rk, ka)#

Result of a DKI fit, as maps on the image grid (zero outside the fitted mask).

Each map has the spatial shape (X, Y, Z) of the DWI, with a last axis for dt, kt, pe and eigen_vals. Every map is float32, except npd. Diffusivities are in mm^2/s, the inverse of the units of the b-values; kurtosis values are dimensionless.

The apparent kurtosis along a unit vector n is K(n) = MD^2 W(n) / D(n)^2, with D(n) = n^T D n and W(n) the quartic form of the kurtosis tensor.

dt#

The 6 unique elements of the diffusion tensor in the order xx, xy, xz, yy, yz, zz, stored in world coordinates (RAS+).

Type:

numpy.ndarray

kt#

Kurtosis tensor W, containing its 15 unique elements in the order W1111, W2222, W3333, W1112, W1113, W2221, W2223, W3331, W3332, W1122, W1133, W2233, W1123, W2213, W3312.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

s0#

Fitted signal at b = 0, in the units of the DWI.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

fa#

Fractional anisotropy, clipped to [0, 1].

Type:

numpy.ndarray

md#

Mean diffusivity.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

ad#

Axial diffusivity.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

rd#

Radial diffusivity.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

pe#

Principal eigenvector in world coordinates (RAS+); its sign is arbitrary.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

eigen_vals#

Eigenvalues l1 >= l2 >= l3, as fitted (they can be negative).

Type:

numpy.ndarray

npd#

True in the fitted voxels whose diffusion tensor is not positive definite (l3 <= 0), bool. The diffusion maps there are computed from the eigenvalues as fitted, and the kurtosis maps are 0.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

mk#

Mean kurtosis, the mean of K(n) over the sphere.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

ak#

Axial kurtosis, K(n) along the principal eigenvector.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

rk#

Radial kurtosis, the mean of K(n) over the directions perpendicular to the principal eigenvector.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

ka#

Kurtosis anisotropy, the standard deviation of K(n) over the sphere.

Type:

numpy.ndarray

class larmor.dwi.DKIModel(*, fit_method='wls', constrained=True)#

Diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) model, with a diffusion and a kurtosis tensor.

DKI extends the diffusion tensor with a kurtosis tensor W that describes how far the diffusion departs from a Gaussian. It needs at least two non-zero shells. fit returns a DKIFit with the tensors and their maps (FA, MD, MK and others).

References

Jensen JH, Helpern JA, Ramani A, Lu H, Kaczynski K. Diffusional kurtosis imaging: the quantification of non-Gaussian water diffusion by means of magnetic resonance imaging. Magn Reson Med 53 (2005) 1432-1440. Veraart J, Sijbers J, Sunaert S, Leemans A, Jeurissen B. Weighted linear least squares estimation of diffusion MRI parameters: strengths, limitations, and pitfalls. NeuroImage 81 (2013) 335-346.

fit(volume, gtab, mask=None, *, n_jobs=-1)#

Fits the diffusion and kurtosis tensors in every voxel of the mask.

Before the fit, the data are cleaned so that every log is defined. Each voxel’s S0 is estimated with larmor.dwi.estimate_s0. Where that estimate differs from the mean of the voxel’s own b0s (because those b0s are implausible), it replaces the b0 measurements. Every diffusion-weighted measurement is then capped at S0. A voxel without a finite, positive S0 is not fitted and is 0 in every map, and a warning gives the number of such voxels. A measurement that is still not finite and positive is set to 1e-3 S0. Without a b0 in the table nothing is replaced or capped, and such measurements are set to 1e-3 times the largest value of the data.

The constrained fit (Tabesh et al., 2011) keeps the tensors physically plausible. It minimises the same (weighted) sum of squares subject to D(n) >= 0, K(n) >= 0 and K(n) <= 3 / (b_max D(n)), where b_max is the largest b-value of the table, and all shells are fitted. The constraints are imposed along 256 directions n spread over the half sphere, not along the acquired gradient directions. A voxel whose unconstrained solution breaks a constraint is fitted again as a least-squares problem with linear inequality constraints (Lawson and Hanson, 1974); where that finds no solution or does not converge, the voxel keeps its unconstrained fit. For “wls”, the weights are those of the unconstrained weighted fit, with no further reweighting. The upper bound grows as D(n) goes to 0, so voxels with near-zero diffusivity (at the edge of the mask, for example) can give very large kurtosis values. Each voxel is fitted on its own, so the result does not depend on n_jobs.

Parameters:

  • volume (Volume) – Diffusion-weighted volume, shape (X, Y, Z, N).

  • gtab (GradientTable) – Its gradient table, with at least two non-zero shells; the tensors come out in its frame, world coordinates.

  • mask (Volume | ndarray | None) – Voxels to fit (nonzero inside), on the volume’s grid. Defaults to None, which computes a brain mask from the mean b0 volume.

  • n_jobs (int) – Number of threads for the fit, at most the number of cores; -1 uses every core. Defaults to -1.

Returns:

DKIFit – The tensors and their maps, zero outside the mask.

Raises:

ValueError – If the table or the mask does not match the volume, the measurements cannot determine the tensors (fewer than two non-zero shells, or too few directions), if the mask is computed and there is no b0, or if n_jobs is 0 or below -1.

Return type:

DKIFit

References

Tabesh A, Jensen JH, Ardekani BA, Helpern JA. Estimation of tensors and tensor-derived measures in diffusional kurtosis imaging. Magn Reson Med 65 (2011) 823-836. Lawson CL, Hanson RJ. Solving Least Squares Problems. Prentice-Hall, 1974 (chapter 23).

class larmor.dwi.DTIModel(*, fit_method='ols')#

Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) model, which fits a diffusion tensor per voxel.

fit returns a DTIFit with the tensor and its maps (FA, MD, AD, RD and the principal eigenvector).

References

Basser PJ, Mattiello J, LeBihan D. Estimation of the effective self-diffusion tensor from the NMR spin echo. J Magn Reson B 103 (1994) 247-254. Veraart J, Sijbers J, Sunaert S, Leemans A, Jeurissen B. Weighted linear least squares estimation of diffusion MRI parameters: strengths, limitations, and pitfalls. NeuroImage 81 (2013) 335-346.

fit(volume, gtab, mask=None, *, n_jobs=-1)#

Fits the tensor in every voxel of the mask.

Signal values that are not positive or finite are replaced by 1e-3 S0 before taking the log, with the voxel’s S0 from larmor.dwi.estimate_s0; without a b0 in the table, or where that S0 is not positive, by 1e-3 times the largest value of the data. Each voxel is fitted on its own, so the result does not depend on n_jobs.

Parameters:

  • volume (Volume) – Diffusion-weighted volume, shape (X, Y, Z, N).

  • gtab (GradientTable) – Its gradient table, with at least two distinct b-values (a b0 is not needed when a mask is given); the tensor comes out in its frame, world coordinates.

  • mask (Volume | ndarray | None) – Voxels to fit (nonzero inside), on the volume’s grid. Defaults to None, which computes a brain mask from the mean b0 volume.

  • n_jobs (int) – Number of threads for the fit, at most the number of cores; -1 uses every core. Defaults to -1.

Returns:

DTIFit – The tensor and its maps, zero outside the mask.

Raises:

ValueError – If the table or the mask does not match the volume, the measurements cannot determine the tensor and S0 (for example one shell without b0, or fewer than six non-coplanar directions), if the mask is computed and there is no b0, or if n_jobs is 0 or below -1.

Return type:

DTIFit

larmor.dwi.create_brain_mask(data_b0, *, threshold=0.5, morph=7)#

Computes a brain mask from a b0 volume.

The volume is thresholded at an automatic level between background and foreground, multiplied by threshold; the largest connected region is kept, and an erosion and a dilation with a roughly spherical element of morph voxels remove thin structures outside the brain.

Parameters:

  • data_b0 (NDArray[floating]) – The b0 volume, shape (X, Y, Z).

  • threshold (float) – Factor applied to the automatic intensity threshold; lower values give a larger mask. Defaults to 0.5.

  • morph (int) – Size in voxels of the structuring element used for erosion and dilation. Defaults to 7.

Returns:

Boolean mask, shape (X, Y, Z).

Raises:

ValueError – If no voxel is left after thresholding or after the erosion, for example when the object is smaller than the structuring element.

Return type:

NDArray[bool]

larmor.dwi.denoise_mppca(volume, mask=None, *, patch_size=None, n_jobs=-1, progress=False)#

Denoises a diffusion-weighted volume by MP-PCA and estimates its noise level.

MP-PCA (Marchenko-Pastur principal component analysis) uses the redundancy between the volumes of a DWI: in a small neighbourhood, the signal is described by a few principal components and the rest is noise.

Every voxel is denoised in its own patch, centred on it. The result does not depend on the number of threads.

The noise is assumed Gaussian, constant within a patch and uncorrelated, and the Rician bias of magnitude images is not corrected.

Parameters:

  • volume (Volume) – The diffusion-weighted volume, (X, Y, Z, M), with all its volumes (b0s included), as acquired.

  • mask (Volume | ndarray | None) – Voxels to denoise (nonzero inside), on the volume’s grid. Patches still use the voxels outside it. Defaults to None, every voxel.

  • patch_size (int | integer | Sequence[int | integer] | None) – The patch width in voxels, odd, the same along every axis or one per axis; along an axis shorter than that, the whole axis. Defaults to None, the smallest odd width w with w^3 >= M (5 for up to 125 volumes, 7 for up to 343).

  • n_jobs (int) – Number of threads, at most the cores; -1 is all cores. Defaults to -1.

  • progress (bool) – Whether to show a progress bar. Defaults to False.

Returns:

DenoisedDWI – The denoised volume, the noise map sigma and the number of signal components per voxel.

Raises:

  • ValueError – If the volume is not 4D, the mask does not match its grid, patch_size is not odd and positive, n_jobs is 0 or below -1, or the data is complex.

  • TypeError – If patch_size is not an integer or a sequence of integers.

Return type:

DenoisedDWI

References

Veraart J, Novikov DS, Christiaens D, Ades-aron B, Sijbers J, Fieremans E. Denoising of diffusion MRI using random matrix theory. NeuroImage 142 (2016) 394-406. Tournier JD et al. MRtrix3: A fast, flexible and open software framework for medical image processing and visualisation. NeuroImage 202 (2019) 116137.

larmor.dwi.estimate_s0(volume, gtab, mask)#

Returns the signal without diffusion weighting, S0, of every voxel of a mask.

S0 is the mean of a voxel’s b0 measurements. Diffusion weighting can only lower the signal, so a voxel whose mean b0 is at or below the mean of its diffusion-weighted measurements has implausible b0s (dark, or 0, as at the edge of the brain). Such a voxel takes the mean S0 of the plausible mask voxels in its 3x3x3 neighbourhood, or in its 5x5x5 neighbourhood when there is none, and NaN when there is none there either.

Parameters:

  • volume (Volume) – Diffusion-weighted volume, shape (X, Y, Z, N).

  • gtab (GradientTable) – Its gradient table; its b0_mask selects the b0s.

  • mask (ndarray) – Voxels to estimate S0 in, shape (X, Y, Z) bool.

Returns:

S0, shape (X, Y, Z), float64; the mean b0 outside the mask.

Raises:

ValueError – If the table does not match the volume, or has no b0.

Return type:

ndarray

larmor.dwi.remove_gibbs_ringing(volume, *, slice_axis=2, num_shifts=20, window=(1, 3), n_jobs=-1)#

Removes Gibbs ringing from a 3D or 4D volume, slice by slice.

The local sub-voxel shift method (Kellner et al., 2016) re-interpolates each line of a slice at num_shifts sub-voxel shifts on each side of zero, and keeps for each voxel the shift with the least oscillation next to it. Lines wrap around at the edge of the slice. The method assumes full k-space sampling; along a partial Fourier direction some ringing may remain. Each slice is computed on its own, so the result does not depend on the number of threads.

Parameters:

  • volume (Volume) – The image, (X, Y, Z) or (X, Y, Z, N); its slope and intercept are applied.

  • slice_axis (int) – The voxel axis across the acquired slices (0, 1 or 2); the other two span each slice. Defaults to 2.

  • num_shifts (int) – Shifts tried on each side of zero. Defaults to 20.

  • window (tuple[int, int]) – The neighbours, from window[0] to window[1] + 1 voxels from the voxel, over which the oscillation is measured. Defaults to (1, 3).

  • n_jobs (int) – Number of threads, -1 for all cores. Defaults to -1.

Returns:

Volume – The image without ringing, float32, on the same grid.

Raises:

ValueError – If slice_axis is not 0, 1 or 2, num_shifts is below 1, the window is not 0 <= first <= last, n_jobs is 0 or below -1, or the data is complex.

Return type:

Volume

References

Kellner E, Dhital B, Kiselev VG, Reisert M. Gibbs-ringing artifact removal based on local subvoxel-shifts. Magn Reson Med 76 (2016) 1574-1581. Tournier JD et al. MRtrix3: A fast, flexible and open software framework for medical image processing and visualisation. NeuroImage 202 (2019) 116137.
