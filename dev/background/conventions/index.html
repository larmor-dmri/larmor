Conventions#

This page lists the coordinates, units, axis orders and bases that Larmor uses, so that you can check your data against them. Each section names the functions and classes that follow its conventions.

Coordinates and spaces#

Term

Meaning

Voxel coordinates

Indices (i, j, k) along the image’s voxel axes, counted from 0. Index (i, j, k) is the centre of that voxel.

World coordinates

Scanner coordinates in mm, RAS+: x increases to the subject’s right, y to anterior, z to superior.

Affine

The (4, 4) float64 matrix that maps voxel indices to world coordinates. Its last row is [0, 0, 0, 1].

Orientation

For each voxel axis, the world direction it is closest to: ("R", "A", "S") means i increases to the right, j to anterior, k to superior.

Every Volume and Grid carries its affine; the voxel sizes are the lengths of its first three columns. The points of a Tractogram, the positions of Seeds, the directions of a GradientTable and the viewer’s cursor are all in world coordinates, whatever the file’s own convention.

Reading the affine. read_nifti() takes the sform when sform_code > 0, otherwise the qform when qform_code > 0, and otherwise diag(pixdim[1], pixdim[2], pixdim[3], 1). In the qform, a negative qfac in pixdim[0] flips the third axis, and the voxel sizes pixdim[1..3] are taken as positive, with a warning when one is negative. The two codes are kept in Volume.xform_codes. xyzt_units is ignored, and lengths are always taken as mm. read_mif() builds the affine from the header’s transform and vox, and returns the axes in the order of dim whatever their order and direction on disk (the layout).

Writing the affine. write_nifti() writes the affine exactly into the sform and its closest rotation into the qform. It writes the codes in Volume.xform_codes, which are those of the file the volume was read from, those of the reference grid for a resampled image, or 1 (scanner) for a volume made in memory. write_mif() writes layout: +0,+1,+2 (x fastest) and the affine as transform and vox.

Orientation. Volume.orientation gives the orientation codes. to_orientation() and to_closest_canonical() (or lar convert --to-ras) only permute and flip voxel axes and update the affine, so every voxel keeps its world position and nothing is resampled. Reorienting an image never changes its gradient table, which is in world coordinates.

Masks. A mask passed as a Volume to DTIModel.fit, DKIModel.fit, SDModel.fit, estimate_wm_rf() or denoise_mppca() must be on the image’s grid. A mask stored in another orientation of that grid (axes permuted or flipped, the same voxels in the world) is reoriented to the image’s, with a warning, and a mask on another grid is refused. A mask passed as an array must have the image’s spatial shape. Nonzero values are inside.

Gradient tables#

A GradientTable holds one b-value and one direction per volume, in the order of the volumes.

Item

Convention

b-values

s/mm^2

Directions

Unit vectors in world coordinates (RAS+)

Vectors not of unit length

Made unit length, with b multiplied by the squared length: 0.5 at b=2800 becomes 1 at b=700. A vector shorter than 0.1 on a volume above the b=0 threshold is refused, and a volume that the scaling brings to the threshold or below is reported

b=0 volumes

b at or below b0_threshold, 50 s/mm^2 by default

Shells

The other b-values, sorted; a gap larger than shell_epsilon (80 s/mm^2 by default) starts a new shell. A shell’s b-value is the mean of its volumes

Selecting a shell

shell_mask() keeps the b-values within tol of the shell’s (100 s/mm^2 by default)

b-matrix

One row per volume: b * (gx^2, 2 gx gy, 2 gx gz, gy^2, 2 gy gz, gz^2), in world coordinates

Files store the directions in different frames, and the frame argument of read_bvals_bvecs(), write_bvals_bvecs() and load_dwi() (--bvec-frame on the command line) says which one a file uses:

File

Frame

Python

FSL bvecs, frame="fsl" (the default)

The image’s voxel axes (the affine’s columns divided by the voxel sizes), with x negated when their determinant is positive; each converted vector is rescaled to its length in the file, which matters only for an image with shear. Converting needs the image.

read_bvals_bvecs(), write_bvals_bvecs()

bvecs, frame="world"

World coordinates, nothing converted

The same, with frame="world"

MRtrix3 gradient file (.b), MIF dw_scheme

World coordinates, rows x y z b

read_mrtrix_gradients(), write_mrtrix_gradients(), read_mif_gradients()

A bvecs file is read as (3, N) or (N, 3) and written as (3, N); bvals are written as one row. A b-matrix does not store the sign of a direction, so read_b_matrix() returns each with its first non-zero component positive.

See Gradient tables and shells.

Spherical harmonics#

FODs are stored as real spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients along the last axis of a 4D image, in world coordinates like the gradient table. A direction is given by theta, its angle from +z, and phi, its angle around z from +x.

Item

Convention

Bases

"tournier" (the default), "descoteaux", "leemans"

Coefficient order

By degree l, then by order m from -l to l

Symmetric FOD

Even degrees only: (lmax + 1)(lmax + 2) / 2 coefficients (1, 6, 15, 28, 45 for lmax 0 to 8)

Asymmetric FOD

All degrees: (lmax + 1)^2 coefficients (4, 9, 16, 25, …)

Telling them apart

By the number of coefficients (sh_layout()); a count that fits both is symmetric

Normalisation

Orthonormal on the sphere. N(l, m) = sqrt((2l + 1) / (4 pi) * (l - |m|)! / (l + |m|)!), and the associated Legendre function P(l, |m|) includes the Condon-Shortley phase

FOD scale

The integral over the sphere, c00 * sqrt(4 pi), is the tissue’s signal fraction relative to S0

lmax of a fit

Even and at least 2, 8 by default; lowered by 2 until the highest shell has at least as many volumes as coefficients

The three bases are defined below in terms of the normalised Legendre part of each harmonic, Y(l, m) = N(l, m) P(l, |m|)(cos theta):

Basis

m < 0

m = 0

m > 0

tournier

sqrt(2) Y(l, m) sin(|m| phi)

Y(l, 0)

sqrt(2) Y(l, m) cos(m phi)

descoteaux

sqrt(2) (-1)^m Y(l, m) cos(|m| phi)

Y(l, 0)

sqrt(2) Y(l, m) sin(m phi)

leemans

tournier with phi replaced by phi - pi/2

tournier

tournier with phi replaced by phi - pi/2

An image file does not record its basis, so the basis must be given whenever an FOD is read: with basis= in Python, with --basis on the command line, and in the FOD’s options when the viewer loads it. SDModel writes symmetric FODs; find_peaks(), the Tracker, lar sh-convert and the viewer read both symmetric and asymmetric ones. convert_sh_basis() converts between the available bases, and fit_sh() fits coefficients to amplitudes on a set of directions.

The peaks of a symmetric FOD have z >= 0, while those of an asymmetric FOD keep their sign. lar peaks writes each peak as its unit direction times its amplitude, with three values per peak along the last axis.

See FODs and Peaks.

Tractograms#

A Tractogram holds the points of all streamlines as one (P, 3) float32 array in world mm, with (S + 1,) int64 offsets, so that streamline s is points[offsets[s]:offsets[s + 1]]. Values per point are in point_data (first axis P) and values per streamline in streamline_data (first axis S).

File

Points on disk

Python

TCK

World mm, float32, a NaN row after each streamline and an Inf row at the end. No grid, no values.

read_tck(), write_tck()

TRK

TrackVis voxel mm: mm from the corner of the first voxel, along the axes of the header’s voxel_order. The header’s vox_to_ras, dim and voxel_size convert them to world mm. Scalars become point_data and properties streamline_data.

read_trk(), write_trk()

VTK, .fib

World mm, in RAS or LPS: the format does not fix which, and the reader converts LPS to RAS. The space comes from the space argument (--vtk-space in lar convert) when it is given, else from a SPACE=RAS or SPACE=LPS tag in the title line, else it is taken as RAS. The writer writes RAS with the tag SPACE=RAS. POINT_DATA and CELL_DATA arrays become point_data and streamline_data.

read_vtk(), write_vtk()

TSF

One value per point, in the order of the points, a NaN after each streamline. Paired with its TCK by the header’s timestamp.

read_tsf(), write_tsf()

A TRK file of version 2 is placed by its own vox_to_ras; one of version 1, or without vox_to_ras, needs a reference grid. A TRK without voxel_order is read as LPS. Writing a TRK needs a reference grid (the reference argument, or the tractogram’s own), which sets dim, voxel_size, vox_to_ras and voxel_order.

Point order. The Tracker keeps every point it steps to inside the image and the tracking mask. A step that would leave the image or the mask ends the streamline without adding its point, so every point of a streamline lies inside both. A streamline tracked one way starts at its seed. One tracked both ways (the default) runs from the end reached along the seed’s first direction, through the seed, to the end reached along the opposite direction. Seed files (read_seeds(), write_seeds()) hold world mm.

See Tractography and Loading and saving data.

The viewer#

Item

Convention

Display

Neurological (RAS) by default: the subject’s right on the right of the screen. Radiological (LAS) puts it on the left in the axial and coronal views; the sagittal view is the same in both. Set it with Display convention in Settings, or Viewer.set_convention and Convention.

Direction colours

Red, green and blue are the absolute x, y and z components of a unit direction in world coordinates: red for left-right, green for anterior-posterior, blue for superior-inferior. This holds for direction maps, streamlines (the tangent at each point), peaks and FOD glyphs. A direction map’s channels are clipped to [0, 1], and can be weighted by a map such as FA.

Crosshair readout

The cursor’s position in world mm, and the main volume’s name, nearest voxel (i, j, k, with the volume index of a 4D image) and value there, with the scaling applied, interpolated if the volume is shown interpolated. Outside the volume’s grid there is no voxel or value. Viewer.readout returns the same.

Slice numbers

Counted from 0, as voxel indices of the main volume.

See The 2D views and Settings.
