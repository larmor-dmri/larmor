Dissecting a bundle#

The Dissect workspace (Ctrl+3, Cmd+3 on macOS) takes you from a diffusion-weighted image, a FOD file or a tractogram to a bundle: the streamlines of a whole-brain tractogram that pass through regions you draw. Its steps are listed on the left, each with a tick once it is done and a line that says where it stands, and the page of the step you are on is on the right, with the fields that matter, a More fold for the rest, and Back and Next at the bottom.

Start, FODs and Track#

The Start step asks what you have. Starting from a tractogram reads it and goes straight to the Regions step; the FODs and Track steps then stay in the list, greyed out and marked as skipped. Starting from a FOD file takes you to the FODs step, where you choose the file’s SH basis, which the file does not record, and load it. Starting from a DWI opens it as a volume and takes you to the FODs step with the DWI filled in, and with its bvals and bvecs files when they sit beside it with the same name.

When the viewer already holds tractograms, FODs or DWIs read from a file, each Start button opens a menu that lists them above From a file…, so nothing has to be loaded twice. Choosing a FOD that is open goes straight to the Track step, since it already has its basis. A file that is already open is not read again, from the Start step or the FODs step: its item is used.

The FODs step either loads a FOD file or estimates the FODs from the DWI. Estimating needs a brain mask, chosen from the overlays and volumes loaded, or made with New mask…, and offers the method and the highest SH degree on the page; the grey matter and CSF responses, response files for CSD and MSMT-CSD, the solver, its penalty weight, the inner-shell weight and the frame of the bvecs file are under More. The defaults are those of lar fod, and the same choices give the same FODs. The fit runs in the background with a progress bar and Cancel, and the FODs (and the tissue fractions of a multi-tissue fit) are added as items.

An mFOD fit, or any fit with a NODDI grey matter, gives two FODs, one for white matter and one for grey matter. The tracker follows a single FOD, so Merge FODs under More makes them one, as lar fod --fod-merge does. The weighted sum (mFOD-WS, the default) multiplies each FOD by its tissue fraction and adds them, so the white matter FOD leads in the white matter and the grey matter FOD near the cortex. Majority keeps, in each voxel, the FOD of the tissue with the larger fraction. The CSF fraction counts in neither. With none, both FODs are added as items. Whenever there is more than one FOD, the Track step asks which one to track.

The Track step tracks the whole brain through those FODs, with the library’s defaults for every field left on auto. Under More you choose where streamlines start, the whole seed mask until the number of streamlines is kept or a number of seeds in every voxel, the seed and tracking masks, which default to the mask of the fit, and the random seed. The run is kept with the tractogram it made, with every setting and the seed it drew, so that it can be run again.

Regions and Save#

The Regions step lists the regions with a tick for those that filter the tractogram, and says how many streamlines they keep. The Sphere ROI (O), Box ROI (B) and Label region (L) tools place regions in the views, and regions are moved and resized only in this workspace; they are still drawn, and still filter, in View. Open regions… adds the regions of a regions file saved earlier.

When an overlay shown as labels is loaded, such as a FreeSurfer aparc+aseg, the top of the Regions page shows a Parcellation section that keeps streamlines by its labels. From A to B keeps the streamlines with one end in the labels of set A and the other end in those of set B; an end counts for a label when a voxel of it lies within the End radius (4 mm by default), so a streamline that stops just short of the grey matter still counts. Stay within keeps the streamlines that never leave the labels of set A. Add a label with + label, which searches the names of the parcellation’s lookup table, or pick the Label region tool (L) and click the label on a slice; Shift+click removes it, and the button beside each set chooses the set the tool adds to. The labels chosen are drawn on the slices and as surfaces in 3D, the count of kept streamlines follows every change, and the label region combines with the spheres, boxes and masks like any other region.

Making a mask#

Every mask field in Dissect, and the Regions page, has a New mask… link that opens the mask sheet. On its left you choose what the mask is made from: the brain mask of the DWI, as lar mask computes it from the mean b0, with the b-values taken from the Start step; a threshold of an image, such as an FA map above 0.2; or labels of a parcellation. The middle shows the mask on one slice of the image as you change the settings, with its number of voxels and its volume, and scrolling moves through the slices. On the right you can clean the mask up, by dilating or eroding it by a number of voxels, filling its holes and keeping only its largest piece, and combine it with another mask by union, intersection or subtraction. Create mask adds it as an overlay and puts it in the field the sheet was opened from; from the Regions page it is added as a mask region that filters the tractogram. Save… also writes it to a file. Painting a mask by hand is not available yet.

The Save step writes, into the folder you choose and under the name you give, the kept streamlines as name.tck, the regions as name.regions.json, and, when the tractogram was tracked here, the run as name_run.json. The page lists every file it will write and asks before replacing one. It also lists the items the dissection made that have no file yet, such as the FODs, the tissue fractions, the whole-brain tractogram and new masks, each with a tick box; those the regions file or the run record name are always saved, since the files must point to them.

To reproduce a bundle, open the whole-brain tractogram, start the Dissect workspace from it and use Open regions… on the regions file: the same regions keep the same streamlines. The run record holds the input files and the settings of the run, and lar track with those files, settings and --seed tracks the same whole-brain tractogram again.

Every item Dissect makes can also be saved on its own: the FODs and Track pages have a Save… button once their result exists, and File > Save As… saves the item selected in the scene tree. Saving a session while some items have no file asks whether to save them next to the session first, as the session can only reopen items from files.
