Exporting images and videos#

The Export sheet saves what the views show as an image or a video, for a paper, a slide or a web page. Open it with Export… in the header, with File > Export… or with Ctrl+Shift+S (Cmd+Shift+S on macOS). The sheet is a window of its own: a preview of the export is on its left and the choices are on its right, and the preview follows the views while the sheet is open.

What chooses what to save: One image, 2D views as separate images or 3D rotation video. For one image, View chooses one of the four views, or All views as the window lays them out: only the enlarged view if one is enlarged, the view shown in the 1 layout, and otherwise the four views in a 2 x 2 grid, also for the 1 + 3 layout. It starts on the view last clicked each time the sheet opens. Image size says how large the image or each frame will be.

Presets#

A preset decides how the export is drawn. It sets the size of the image, which the views share when the image shows all of them, or keeps the size the views have on the screen, a scale that multiplies that size, the background colour, whether the information overlay is drawn, and whether the background is transparent. It can also give the items other options while the export is drawn, such as tubes for the tractograms, and hold a position of the 3D camera. Those options apply only to the export: the views on the screen are as they were once it is saved. The small table under Preset shows the chosen preset’s values.

The viewer comes with four presets.

  • Screen draws the views as they are on the screen, at the screen’s size and resolution.

  • Figure draws them at twice the resolution on a white background, without the overlay and with a transparent background, for a paper.

  • Talk draws them at twice the resolution on a black background, without the overlay, with tractograms as tubes with ambient occlusion, for a slide.

  • Website draws images of 1800 x 1200 pixels on a black background, without the overlay, with tractograms as tubes of 0.25 mm with ambient occlusion, the smoothest FOD glyphs and blocky slices, and the 3D view seen from the subject’s front right, 12 degrees above the horizontal.

Edit preset… changes the chosen preset’s name, size, scale, background, overlay and transparency, and can save the 3D view’s camera as it is now. The preset is saved as a TOML file in the export_presets folder beside the settings (see Settings), so it is there the next time; a preset saved under the name of one of the four replaces it.

Camera chooses where the 3D view is seen from: the preset’s own position, when it has one, the 3D view’s current camera, or the axial, coronal or sagittal position. Transparent background leaves the space around the images see-through, for PNG, TIFF and WebP; it starts as the preset says. Also save the session beside the image writes a session file with the image’s name, such as figure.lar.json beside figure.png, so that you can open the same view again later (see Sessions).

Save… asks for a file, which can be PNG, TIFF or, for an image without transparency, JPEG, and Cancel closes the sheet.

Separate views and rotation videos#

2D views as separate images saves the axial, coronal and sagittal views at once, each in its own file, so saving figure.png writes figure_axial.png, figure_coronal.png and figure_sagittal.png. If any of them exists, the sheet asks once before replacing them.

3D rotation video saves the 3D view turning round as an animated WebP or GIF. It starts from the camera the sheet chooses, and three fields set the video: Speed in degrees per second (30 by default; a negative speed turns the other way), Duration in seconds (12 by default, one full turn at 30 degrees per second) and Frames per second (30 by default). A progress window follows the recording, and its Cancel stops it without writing a file. A transparent background needs WebP, because GIF has no partial transparency. GIF also has fewer colours and plays at most 50 frames per second, so above that rate the file dialog offers only WebP.

From Python, without a window, viewer.export("Website", "slide.png", ViewKind.THREE_D) saves one view with a preset, viewer.export_views("Figure", "figure.png") saves the three 2D views and viewer.export_rotation("Talk", "turn.webp") saves the video. viewer.screenshot(None, "figure.png", scale=2), viewer.screenshot_views and viewer.record_rotation take the size, scale and switches themselves instead of a preset.
