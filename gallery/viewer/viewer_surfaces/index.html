Surfaces in the viewer#

This example builds a synthetic head and a folded surface inside it, with a curvature-like value per vertex. It saves screenshots of the 3D view with the surface coloured by that value, and of the axial view with the surface’s cut line over the head.

A surface, such as the white matter surface FreeSurfer makes, is drawn in the 3D view as a shaded mesh and in the 2D views as the line where each slice cuts it. Its values per vertex, such as its curvature, can colour it.

import numpy as np

from larmor.types import Surface, Volume
from larmor.viewer import Category, OptionName, Viewer, ViewKind

A synthetic head#

The head is a 2 mm image of an ellipsoid centred at the world origin, as in the other examples. World coordinates are in mm, RAS+.

shape = (90, 108, 90)
affine = np.diag([2.0, 2.0, 2.0, 1.0])
affine[:3, 3] = -np.array(shape)  # half the extent in mm: the centre at 0
i, j, k = np.indices(shape, dtype=np.float32)
x, y, z = (2 * i / shape[0] - 1, 2 * j / shape[1] - 1, 2 * k / shape[2] - 1)
r = np.sqrt((x / 0.8) ** 2 + (y / 0.9) ** 2 + (z / 0.85) ** 2)
head = Volume(np.where(r < 0.9, 600 - 300 * r, 0).astype(np.float32), affine)

A folded surface#

The surface is shaped like one hemisphere: an ellipsoid, long front to back and flattened on its inner (right) side. It is meshed by latitude and longitude, and its radius is rippled into folds. A surface holds its vertices in world coordinates (mm) and its faces as vertex indices, counter-clockwise seen from outside. The ripple, negated, plays the part of FreeSurfer’s curvature: positive in the folds’ troughs (sulci), negative on their crests (gyri). It is stored as the values “curv”, the name FreeSurfer’s curvature files get.

rows, columns = 120, 240
theta = np.linspace(0, np.pi, rows)[:, None]  # from the top down
phi = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, columns, endpoint=False)[None, :]  # around
ripple = np.sin(17 * phi + 3 * np.cos(4 * theta)) * np.sin(
    13 * theta + 2 * np.sin(3 * phi)
)
radius = 1 + 0.04 * ripple
sx = np.sin(theta) * np.cos(phi)
sy = np.sin(theta) * np.sin(phi)
sz = np.cos(theta) * np.ones_like(phi)
sx = np.where(sx > 0, 0.35 * sx, sx)  # the flat inner side faces the midline
points = np.stack(
    [-30 + 35 * radius * sx, 70 * radius * sy, 5 + 55 * radius * sz], axis=-1
).reshape(-1, 3)
faces = []
for row in range(rows - 1):
    for column in range(columns):
        a = row * columns + column
        b = row * columns + (column + 1) % columns
        faces += [[a, a + columns, b], [b, a + columns, b + columns]]
surface = Surface(points, np.array(faces), vertex_data={"curv": -ripple.ravel() / 2})

The viewer and its items#

Surfaces are added like the other items; load reads a FreeSurfer surface, such as lh.white with its lh.curv beside it, or a GIfTI surf.gii file. A surface whose name contains “white” is drawn in yellow, and a surface with “curv” values starts coloured by them.

viewer = Viewer()
# The screenshots hide the crosshair and the text, so they show only the data.
viewer.toggle_crosshair()
viewer.toggle_overlay()
head_item = viewer.add(head, Category.VOLUMES, name="head")
item = viewer.add(surface, Category.SURFACES, name="lh.white")

Coloured by curvature#

The curvature is drawn in grey from -0.3 to 0.3, with the troughs dark and the crests light. The head would hide the surface in the 3D view, so it is hidden for this screenshot.

viewer.set(head_item.id, OptionName.VISIBLE, False)
viewer.screenshot(ViewKind.THREE_D, "viewer_surfaces_curvature.png", size=(600, 600))

The cut line#

The 2D views draw the line where the slice cuts the surface. Here it is drawn in the surface’s own colour, after the colouring by values is switched off. Cut lines are how a surface is checked against its T1 image, and the Cut lines option turns them off.

viewer.set(head_item.id, OptionName.VISIBLE, True)
viewer.set(item.id, OptionName.COLOUR_BY, None)
viewer.screenshot(ViewKind.AXIAL, "viewer_surfaces_cut_line.png", size=(600, 600))
print(f"{surface.num_vertices} vertices, {surface.num_faces} faces")

28800 vertices, 57120 faces
